The valley California’s Cabernet came from was just named region of the year. Its best bottle is $20.

Winey Opines · 19 October 2026

Short answer: the question is whether an award for a region makes its wine worth more, and the answer is that an award changes the price before it changes the wine, so the window to buy Livermore at Livermore prices is open now and closes behind the headline. The Wente Wetmore Vineyard Cabernet at $20 is the find. The $14 Southern Hills is fair. And the next find in the valley will not say Cabernet Sauvignon on the label at all.

What happened

On October 15 Wine Enthusiast gave Livermore Valley its 2026 Wine Star Award for American Wine Region of the Year. The valley is forty minutes east of San Francisco, has about fifty wineries, most of them family-owned, and the citation talks about quality, growth and the visitor experience, in that order. Brandi Lombardi, who runs the Livermore Valley Wine Community, said, “We’ve raised the bar for what’s in the glass without changing the experience that brings people here.” Tonya Pitts, the Wine Enthusiast reviewer who covers it, said, “It is a region that knows where it came from, and is not afraid to move forward.”

Read that second sentence twice, because it is doing two jobs. “Knows where it came from” is the history, which is the best in California. “Not afraid to move forward” is the admission that the history is not what the valley plans to sell you next. In 2025 the region named Cabernet Franc and Sauvignon Blanc its signature grapes, on the strength of a 2022 UC Davis study of its soils and climate. Not Cabernet Sauvignon. Keep that in mind at the shelf.

Where California Cabernet came from

Wente and Concannon were both founded in 1883, and they are still there, which makes Wente, by its own description, the oldest continuously operated family-owned winery in the country. In 1889 a dry white from Charles Wetmore’s Cresta Blanca, down the road, won the Grand Prix at the Paris Exposition, when nobody in Europe believed California could make wine. By the early 1960s the valley had as many wine grapes in the ground as Napa.

Then the two valleys went opposite ways. Napa became Napa. Livermore became a suburb, and today it has about 4,400 acres of vines, roughly a tenth of Napa’s. But in 1965, before the houses, Jim Concannon worked with UC Davis to take cuttings from one old Cabernet vine on the property, the one the family still calls the Mother Vine, and certify them as clones 7, 8 and 11. By Concannon’s own count, those three clones are in about 80 percent of the Cabernet Sauvignon planted in California. Wente makes the same kind of claim about Chardonnay: three quarters of the state’s Chardonnay vines trace to the Wente clone. Both numbers come from the families, not from an auditor, and I believe the shape of both. When you drink a $90 Napa Cabernet, odds are the vine was a Livermore vine before it was a Napa vine.

Why it is cheap

Three reasons, and none of them is that the wine is bad.

Nobody outside the East Bay has heard of it. Fifty wineries selling mostly at the door to people who drove out from Oakland and Pleasanton do not set a national price. Only Wente and Concannon have real distribution, and Concannon has belonged since 2002 to The Wine Group, the company behind Franzia, which has never seen the point of charging more for anything.

The label hides it. Livermore sits inside two bigger appellations, San Francisco Bay and Central Coast, and a lot of the valley’s wine leaves wearing one of those names instead of its own. A bottle that says Livermore Valley on the front is the one made by somebody proud of it.

Napa set the comparison. In the twelve months to last November, a bottle of Cabernet shipped direct from a Napa winery averaged more than $150. The Wente Wetmore Vineyard Cabernet, from a single Livermore vineyard, with a 93-point score from the Tasting Panel, is $19.97 at wine.com this week, marked down from $35. That is a Napa score at a Lodi price, and the discount tells you the market has not read the news yet.

I will be honest about the rest of the valley, because the award will not be. It is a warm place and a lot of its Cabernet is soft, ripe and ordinary, the kind of bottle that is pleasant at the tasting bar and forgettable at home. The region knows this, which is why it is betting its name on Cabernet Franc, which keeps its acid in heat where Cabernet Sauvignon goes flabby. Steven Kent Mirassou, the sixth generation of a family that has made wine in California since before Lincoln, has rebuilt his whole winery around the grape, a dozen Cab Francs a year and no new oak. That is where the valley’s price will move first.

What to do with the bottle in front of you

Wente Wetmore Vineyard Cabernet Sauvignon 2023, about $20. Buy it, and buy more than one. Single vineyard, real valley on the label, a score that would cost you $75 an hour’s drive north. It will drink well for five years. This is the bottle to put in front of a friend who thinks $20 Cabernet means Lodi.

Wente Southern Hills Cabernet 2023, about $14. Fair, not a find. The valley’s weeknight wine, 90 points from the people who just gave the valley its award, blended across the estate rather than from one site. Drink it within two years. If the Wetmore is on the shelf next to it for six dollars more, the six dollars are the best money you will spend that day.

Anything from the valley that says Cabernet Franc. The small houses sell theirs for what an ordinary Napa Cabernet costs, and that is the one price in Livermore that is going up. If you want to taste what the award was really about, this is it. Buy one now and one in three years and compare the labels; the second one will cost more.

The general rule, for any region that wins something: the award is announced in October and the new price list is printed in January. The bottle on the shelf this month was priced by a valley nobody was watching. Next year’s will be priced by the American Wine Region of the Year. Same vines, same families, same wine. Buy from the first list.

Straight answers

Is Livermore Valley wine any good?

The best of it is very good and underpriced; a lot of it is soft, ordinary Cabernet sold at the tasting-room door. Look for Livermore Valley on the front label, a single vineyard name, or the words Cabernet Franc.

What is Livermore Valley known for?

Two of California’s oldest wineries, Wente and Concannon, both from 1883; the Cabernet clones 7, 8 and 11 that, by Concannon’s count, are in 80 percent of California Cabernet; and the Wente Chardonnay clone. In 2025 it named Cabernet Franc and Sauvignon Blanc its signature grapes.

What is the best Livermore Valley Cabernet under $20?

The Wente Wetmore Vineyard Cabernet Sauvignon 2023, at $19.97 on wine.com this week, 93 points from the Tasting Panel. The Southern Hills at $14 is the fair weeknight bottle.

Why was Livermore Valley named Wine Region of the Year?

Wine Enthusiast cited quality, growth, a largely family-owned community of about fifty wineries, investment in winemaker education and lab testing, and the visitor experience. The award is presented at the 2026 Wine Star Awards.

Livermore Valley or Napa?

Napa for the name and the drive. Livermore for the same vines at a seventh of the price. Napa Cabernet shipped from the winery averaged more than $150 a bottle last year; the best Livermore bottle on the shelf is $20.
