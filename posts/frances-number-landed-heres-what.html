Winey Opines · No. 5

France's number landed. Here's what it means for your next bottle.

Winey Opines · 23 September 2026

Short answer: France’s agriculture ministry says the country will make 33.9 million hectolitres of wine this year, about 4.5 billion bottles. That’s 6 percent under last year, 17 percent under the five-year average, the third weak harvest in a row, and one of the smallest in thirty years. Champagne is the wreck: down by about half. The rest of the country is short but standing, and one region actually grew. Now the part nobody prints: the first number from this ministry is usually the high number.

The number, and its history

Agreste, the statistics office, was supposed to publish in early August and pushed the date to September because it couldn’t see the crop clearly through the heat. The number it finally published is 33.9 million hectolitres. It calls the figure provisional, with “significant regional disparities,” and it should. Last year the same office opened at 40 to 42.5 million, cut to 37.4 in September, cut again to 36.2 in November, and the final declared crop came in at 34.4. Every revision went the same direction. So read 33.9 as the ceiling of a range whose floor nobody knows yet, and don’t be surprised if the floor is what gets declared in spring.

Why

The summer was France’s hottest on record. Spring had looked good, with clean fruit set and little disease, and then drought settled over the whole country and heatwaves burned leaves, scalded bunches and shrivelled grapes on the vine. Picking started at least a week earlier than last year almost everywhere, and two weeks earlier in parts of Bordeaux. Small berries, less juice. That’s the whole mechanism; there’s no mystery in it.

Champagne, halved

The ministry puts Champagne down 49 percent on 2025 and 47 percent below its five-year average, the steepest fall of any region. Two things to hold onto before you do anything about it. First, Champagne is the one region built for exactly this: the houses hold reserve wine from earlier years by law and by habit, and non-vintage Champagne is a blend across years by design. A halved 2026 shows up in the bottles as a thinner reserve for years to come, not as an empty shelf this Christmas. Second, watch for the shortage to be used as the reason for a price rise that was coming anyway. The 2026 crop will be in a bottle you buy in 2029 at the earliest; a price increase this winter is about something else.

The south grew

Languedoc-Roussillon is forecast up 5 percent on last year, still 8 percent under its average, but up. That’s the region that makes most of France’s honest, unfamous, region-and-grape wine, and it’s the region the glut reached first. If you took my advice last time and bought south, this number says keep doing it. The shortage is in the north; the value is still in the south.

The verdict

Don’t panic-buy Champagne; the houses did that for you years ago, and they call it reserve. Don’t read 33.9 as final; read it as the top of the range. Do keep buying from the parts of France the heat spared and the surplus filled. And when someone tells you 2026 is a great vintage because the yields were tiny, remember that small berries make concentrated wine and cooked berries make tired wine, and this summer made both. The names that picked cool and early will surface. Wait for them.

What I don’t know: what the final number will be, and neither does Agreste until the declarations come in. Whether Champagne’s houses dip into reserve quietly or announce it. And what any of it tastes like; it’s still fermenting.

Straight answers

How big is France’s 2026 harvest?

The first official estimate is 33.9 million hectolitres, 6 percent below 2025 and 17 percent below the five-year average, provisional.

Is Champagne really down by half?

The ministry says 49 percent below last year. The houses hold reserve wine for exactly this; expect thinner reserves later, not empty shelves now.

Will French wine get more expensive?

The famous names will use the year as a reason. The south, which grew, has no reason at all.

Is a tiny harvest a great vintage?

Sometimes. Concentration and cooking come from the same heat. Read it bottle by bottle when it lands.
